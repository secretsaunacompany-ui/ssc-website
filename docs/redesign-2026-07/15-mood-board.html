Scroll

A photograph, held long enough to be believed. Then everything else, quietly.

What just happened

You arrived on a full screen of photograph. No headline over it, no button, no logo competing for the frame. The page waited. Then it let you in when you asked.

That is the whole proposal. Everything below is detail.

The scroll

Chunks, not a wall.

Each section arrives on its own. Image, then a held breath, then words. The reader is never handed six things at once.

This is the compression-and-release principle already written into the SSC build language, applied to a page instead of a room. Narrow, then wide. Dense, then empty. It is why the sections below are further apart than feels normal, and why nothing shares a screen with anything else.

The correction

The site already tries this. The intent was right; the execution took the scroll away from the visitor for up to ten seconds. Same instinct, no hostage-taking: content fades up as you arrive, and the first gesture always works.

The voice

Someone specific, saying something true.

This came out of a conversation, not a copy deck:

The whole thing came about because of our love for the hidden saunas in the woods around Squamish, and wanting to recreate that experience once our own backyard had filled with people. So we put one on a trailer and took it to the river.

In most work you end up a single cog. Here we play every role. It means wearing a lot of hats, but you start a conversation, draw the thing, build it, and hand it over finished. That is the satisfying part.

Why it works

No superlatives, no "passion for excellence." It names a real place, a real annoyance, and a real trade-off. Storm cannot run this paragraph. That is the only test that matters.

Photography

Three scales, and one of them is missing.

Establishing (the building in its landscape), atmosphere (light, steam, interior), and detail (grain, joinery, hardware). The library is strong on the first two. The third is thin, and it is the one that separates a builder from a catalogue.

Add hands. Charring cedar, fitting a joint, choosing a board. A hand in frame turns a product shot into evidence.

Delivery note

Full-bleed plates get room and no caption. A photograph carrying a caption reads as a receipt; a photograph alone reads as a work. Captions live in an index, not under the image.

Rendering note

This page substitutes a system serif for Cormorant Garamond, which cannot load here. Character is close: old-style, high contrast, architectural. The real face is lighter still.

The one change to make on the live site is scale, not family. Navigation currently shouts at 1.1rem uppercase; it should sit at 0.85rem. Uppercase or large, never both.

The correction

Secondary text sits at #C0C0C0 today — a cool silver beside a warm accent. Moving it to #9A9590 puts the whole neutral ramp in the same temperature as the wood. Small change, and the page stops feeling faintly mismatched.

Separately: #666 currently carries small text on black. That fails contrast and has to go up regardless of taste.

Motion

Two systems. Not six.

Everything on this page moves in exactly two ways, and you have already seen both.

Keep

  • One load-in The page settles once. Nothing is withheld.
  • Scroll reveals Fade and rise, 28px, firing once as a section arrives. Grid children stagger 120ms apart.
  • Hover grammar Lift, a warmer border, image scale. Consistent on every card.

Cut

  • Scroll-lock intro Two gestures owed before the page responds.
  • Four parallax variants Fighting an 0.8s CSS transition and smearing.
  • Infinite 20s zoom Nothing changed, so nothing should move.
  • Instant snap panels FAQ and mobile menu jump while everything else eases.
The test

Is this adding information, or adding atmosphere? Reveals earn their place because they pace the reading. Parallax does not: it decorates a page that already has photography doing the work.

Reference

The register, and who already holds it.

Three outside the trade, two inside it. The gap is the opportunity: no sauna builder is working in this language.

George Nakashima Woodworkers
nakashimawoodworkers.com

Stark interface, zero applied texture, photography carrying one hundred percent of the material warmth. The lesson: the more real craft you have to show, the less the interface needs to simulate it.

normcph.com

Soft minimalism. Poetic without tipping into corny — the line you named. Whitespace treated as a material and a deliberately slow pace.

The architecture-studio register exactly. Monochrome, content-led, purposeful transitions only. They detect reduced-motion and say so.
