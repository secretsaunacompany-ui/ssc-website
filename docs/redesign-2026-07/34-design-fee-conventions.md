# 34 — Design Fee Conventions: How to Gate Design Work

**Author:** David (sales & BD specialist)
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Question (Lee, verbatim):** "I do end up doing free consultations/estimates though perhaps I should be gating this somewhat. I guess providing designs should come with a deposit that gets integrated into the final cost. Let's research the competitors' language and figure out exactly how these charges should be formatted."
**Answers:** fact-gathering §A2 (consultation) and §A3 (design and planning) in `20-fact-gathering-questions.md`.

---

## Headline answer

The convention Lee is groping toward already has a name and a settled structure across custom trades: **free consultation and site visit, then a paid design deposit that is credited in full against the build if the client proceeds, and kept (not refunded) if they don't.** Two sauna companies already do it verbatim ($350–$600). Design-build remodelers do it at ~2% of project value. Custom furniture makers do it at $200 flat. Nobody at SSC's direct BC competitor tier publishes a design fee — which makes the *framing* matter more than the number, and also means SSC would not be matching a market norm the prospect has already seen from Storm or Theraluxe. The fee has to be introduced as a natural stage of the process, not as a toll.

## 2. Language patterns (what the wording actually does)

Four distinct patterns show up, each solving a different sales problem:

**Pattern A — "Fully credited if you proceed" (the dominant one).**
DripSaunas: *"The design fee is fully credited toward the total project cost if you proceed with the build."*
This is the sentence Lee described from instinct. It reframes the fee as a pre-payment, not a cost. Every serious adjacent trade uses a variant of it.

**Pattern B — "Non-refundable, but treated as a deposit."**
Peterson MADE: *"Although this fee is non-refundable, the full amount will be treated as a deposit and applied as a credit toward your total commission."* Haven of Heat: *"non-refundable once work begins."*
Note the sequencing: the non-refundable clause is always stated second, softened, and paired with the credit. Nobody leads with "non-refundable."

**Pattern C — "Places your project on our schedule."**
Peterson MADE: *"Payment of this fee will place your project on Peterson MADE's schedule."* Storm's equivalent is "10% to enter queue." The fee buys queue position, not just drawings — this converts the charge from a toll into a commitment device (Cialdini's consistency principle, and genuine scarcity for a 6-builds-a-year shop).

**Pattern D — Drawings ownership, two schools.**
Hard school (McCadden template): *"all plans and specifications... will remain the property of [builder] until a Construction Contract... is reached. No plans will be given to owners during the design process."* Protects against the client shopping your drawings to a cheaper builder.
Soft school (Litchfield): *"If you're not ready to move forward, you'll still receive your drawings."* Treats the fee as buying a deliverable, which makes it easier to charge and feels fairer, but accepts the shop-around risk.

**Pattern E — Make it standard, not negotiable.**
Landscape practitioner consensus: *"include it in your marketing materials so it feels standard, not optional."* The fee that appears mid-conversation feels like a toll; the fee printed on the website feels like the process.

## 3. The credit mechanism

How "comes off the final price" is structured across the sample:

- **Credit trigger:** signing the build contract (McCadden: "authorized by the owner... at meeting #3") or placing the materials/build order (Haven of Heat, DripSaunas). The credit appears as a line-item deduction on the first invoice or against the first construction deposit (Phx: "credited towards your remodel costs on the initial proposal").
- **If the client walks:** the fee is **kept**, universally, in every source found. No one refunds it. The polite framing is Peterson's "you will forfeit the full value" buried in terms, or Haven of Heat's "non-refundable once work begins." Nobody found offers partial refunds — partial refunds invite negotiation.
- **Drawings on walk-away:** split as per Pattern D above. The McCadden hard school retains everything; Litchfield hands over drawings. Middle ground (common in practice, and what I recommend): client keeps the concept PDFs they were shown; builder retains working files and build rights to the design.
- **Expiry:** rarely stated, but worth adding — an uncapped credit is an open liability. A 12-month validity is standard practice in adjacent trades and painless to include.
- **Fit with SSC's 30/30/30/10:** the design deposit sits *before* and *inside* the first 30% — i.e., it becomes part of deposit #1 when the contract signs. That keeps the published payment schedule untouched: "30/30/30/10, and your design deposit counts toward the first 30%."

## 4. Where the free/paid line sits

The dominant convention at SSC's price point, across every trade sampled:

1. **Free:** first conversation (call, email, showroom/experience visit).
2. **Free or cheap:** site visit. Local free; distant sometimes charged. SSC's website already promises "Site Visits Included" (`src/squamish.njk:28`) — keep that; it's a sourced, published promise and a local advantage over Island competitors.
3. **Paid:** the moment drawings start. Concept sketches, SketchUp models, renders, itemized quotes with revisions — this is where Haven of Heat, DripSaunas, Litchfield, McCadden, Peterson, and the landscape/ADU sectors all draw the line.
4. **Included in build price:** permit/construction drawings after contract (McCadden: "Plans for permit and construction will be completed after acceptance of a construction contract").

So Lee's instinct matches the cross-trade norm exactly: talk is free, looking is free, **drawing is paid**. His own precedent already half-exists — the Adam Pospisil quote's "Post-start design revisions: $95/hr" charges for design time after the contract; this extends the same logic before it.

## 5. Recommendation for SSC

**What to call it:** **Design deposit.** Not "retainer" (lawyer word), not "fee" (toll word). "Deposit" already implies it comes back to you. This is the single most important word choice, and it's the word Lee used unprompted.

**Amount:**
- **Residential (S2–S8, $22.5k–$52.5k): $750 flat.** Rationale: at ~2% of an S4 (Litchfield's norm) you'd get $590–$750; sauna-specific precedents run $350–$600 for far less than a SketchUp model plus site-specific layout; at Lee's $60/hr internal labour rate it covers roughly a day and a half of design time, which is honest cost recovery, not profit. Flat beats percentage — one number, no negotiation, fits the "transparent pricing, no games" brand position in `competitor-comparison.md`.
- **Commercial (SC and up, $79.5k+): $2,500,** quoted in the proposal, same credit mechanics. Commercial design work (code, occupancy, mechanical coordination) is a different animal and the pricing-overhaul history (Gatherwell scope creep, three SC builds sold at 35–44% of list) says commercial is exactly where unpaid design bled worst.

**Where the line sits:** Free intro call. Free site visit in the Sea-to-Sky corridor (keep the published promise; site visits beyond the corridor billed at cost, which also quietly qualifies distant tire-kickers). Design deposit due before SketchUp opens. Two revision rounds included; further revisions $95/hr (matches the Pospisil precedent, now formalized).

**Credit mechanics:** Credited in full against the first 30% deposit when the build contract signs. Non-refundable if the project doesn't proceed. Valid 12 months. Client keeps the concept PDFs; SSC retains working files and the right to build the design. (Litchfield's soft school for the deliverable, McCadden's hard school for the files — the middle ground. Handing a prospect nothing after $750 would feel punitive; handing them the SketchUp file gifts your labour to a cheaper builder.)

### Draft copy — website (process page, replacing the design step)

> **01 — Talk.** A call or a coffee. Free, and it commits you to nothing.
>
> **02 — Site visit.** I come see your space anywhere in the Sea-to-Sky corridor. Also free.
>
> **03 — Design.** This is where I start drawing. A $750 design deposit gets you a 3D model of your sauna in your actual space, an itemized quote, and two rounds of revisions. Every dollar of it comes off your build price when you go ahead. It also holds your place in the queue — I build six saunas a year, one at a time.
>
> **04 — Build.** Contract, deposit, and your design deposit becomes the first part of your first payment.

(Voice note for George: contractions, no em dashes, first person — matches the "builder who answers your texts" positioning. The queue sentence does double duty: justifies the deposit and states real scarcity.)

### Draft copy — quote / design agreement (formal)

> **Design deposit: $750** (residential) / **$2,500** (commercial)
> Covers site-specific design: 3D model, layout, heater and ventilation spec, and an itemized fixed quote, with two rounds of revisions. Additional revisions are billed at $95/hr.
> The full deposit is credited against your first construction payment when a build agreement is signed within 12 months. If the project doesn't proceed, the deposit covers the design work completed and isn't refunded. You keep the design PDFs; working files and build rights remain with Secret Sauna Company until a build agreement is in place.

### FAQ line (for the site's FAQ)

> **Why is there a design deposit?** Because a real design takes real time — measuring your site, modelling the sauna, sizing the heater, pricing every board. The deposit means I can give that time to people who are serious, and it all comes off your price anyway. The conversation and the site visit are still free.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

**Risk 1 — It costs deals at the trust-decision moment.** A prospect comparing SSC against Storm or Theraluxe sees no design fee anywhere else in the BC sauna market. At the exact moment they're deciding whether to trust a one-man shop, SSC asks for money first. This is real: it will filter out some genuine buyers who simply bristle, not just tire-kickers.
*Mitigation:* the free call and free site visit stay generous and prominent — the prospect gets two substantial free interactions and a ballpark range before any money changes hands. The deposit is 1.2–3.3% of the purchase; framed as "comes off your price" and "holds your build slot," it reads as commitment, not cost. And publish it (Pattern E) — a fee discovered on the website is process; a fee revealed on a call is a bait-and-switch.

**Risk 2 — Ballpark ambiguity.** If nothing numeric is free, prospects can't self-qualify and will walk before paying to find out they can't afford it. *Mitigation:* the website's published model pricing already does this job; the free consult should end with a verbal range ("your project lands between $X and $Y"). Only the *site-specific* design and fixed quote sit behind the deposit.

**Risk 3 — It reads bureaucratic against the artisan brand.** *Mitigation:* first-person copy, the queue framing, and the word "deposit." Brian Rheeder sells one-at-a-time scarcity successfully at $40k+; SSC's six-a-year reality is the same story. A craftsman charging for drawings is normal; a craftsman with a fee schedule is not. Keep it to one number and three sentences.

**Risk 4 — Commercial prospects at $100k+ may expect proposal work for free** (procurement culture). *Mitigation:* for RFP/institutional work, waive at Lee's discretion case-by-case — the design agreement is a default, not a cage. The history (Valtteri, Oll, Emmanuel SC builds at 35–44% of list) argues the discipline is worth more than the exception.

**Honest bottom line:** at six builds a year, losing one marginal deal to the deposit costs more than a year of collected deposits. The deposit's value isn't the $750s — it's that every design hour Lee spends is spent on a qualified buyer, and that the realized-price rot documented in the pricing overhaul (73% of list on average) started with a relationship where everything was free and every boundary was soft. The design deposit is the first boundary; the ones after it get easier.
