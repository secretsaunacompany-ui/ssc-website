## 1. Photography art direction

The site's entire premise — "a photograph, held long enough to be believed" — puts the whole register on the photography's shoulders. The template can be perfect and the site still fails if the plates don't hold. This section is written to be handed to Lee as-is.

### 1.1 The three-scale system, as a shot list

Nomenclature for the whole system (used in Cloudinary naming, templates, and this doc):
- **E — Establishing.** Building in landscape. The building is small in the frame; the site is the subject.
- **A — Atmosphere.** Interior light, steam, glow through glass, occupied space. The feeling is the subject.
- **D — Detail.** Material and joinery at arm's length or closer. The evidence is the subject.
- **P — Process.** Hands making. A sub-category of D in scale but its own category in meaning — the brand guidelines call it SSC's strongest differentiator and the library has almost none of it.

**Current inventory balance (from the photo library index + live site URLs):** E is strong (Alice Lake ×2 + Squamish River shoots, 1,116 pro frames). A is adequate (interior shots exist but few are graded consistently; the best atmosphere frame on the site is actually the fog plate IMG_2819, which has no sauna in it). D is thin — IMG_2403 (bench grain) and IMG_7991 (cladding-meets-window) are the only two true detail plates in rotation. P is effectively **zero** on the site; the build-documentation folders (S2 Clarke, S4 Caro, S6 Gabe, SC Val…) are phone-shot progress records, not made photographs.

**The shoot fills D and P. Do not re-shoot E.**

### 1.2 The shootable brief (for Lee, no photography background assumed)

**Gear:** the phone that shot the PXL_ frames is enough for P shots; the camera that shot the DSC frames is better for D shots. Whichever you use, use the same one for the whole set so the frames match.

**Ten rules. Follow them literally.**

1. **Shoot in the last two hours before sunset, or on an overcast day, or indoors by window light.** Never midday sun (DSC03223 on the current homepage shows why — hard shadows, blue sky, everything fights). Overcast is not a compromise; it's the easiest light to make wood look expensive.
2. **Turn off flash. Always.**
3. **One subject per frame.** Before you press the button, say out loud what the photo is of. If the answer has an "and" in it, move closer.
4. **Move your feet, don't zoom.** Phone zoom degrades; walking in doesn't. For D shots your feet should be closer than feels natural — arm's length or less.
5. **Hold the camera level and square.** Vertical lines vertical. If you're shooting a wall, stand parallel to it. Crooked frames read amateur faster than anything else.
6. **Tap to focus on the subject, then drag exposure DOWN slightly** (the little sun slider on the phone). A frame that's a bit dark is recoverable; blown-out pale wood is not. Everything on this site sits on near-black — darker frames belong.
7. **For detail shots, put light at a shallow angle across the surface,** not behind you. Side light makes grain three-dimensional; front light flattens it.
8. **For process shots, keep shooting while working — don't pose.** Take 10 frames of the same action; one will be right. Hands dirty, tools real, sawdust left where it falls.
9. **Leave empty space on one side of the subject.** These images get text set beside them and get cropped three ways (§1.4); a centered subject dies in the crop. Put the subject on the left or right third.
10. **Wipe the lens.** Shop dust on a phone lens is the difference between "atmosphere" and "smudge."

**Quantity target:** 8–10 keepers per category (D and P). Shoot 10× that. Cull hard: if a frame needs an explanation, it's out.

**What to avoid:** midday sun; faces looking at camera; branded clothing other than SSC; visible clutter (cords, coffee cups, phones) unless it's honest workshop context in a P frame; portrait-mode fake blur (it smears wood grain edges — use real proximity for blur); any filter applied on the phone.

### 1.3 Grading direction

Direct inspection of the live frames shows the actual problem: **the library has no shared grade.** IMG_2403 (bench) carries a heavy amber/orange cast; IMG_7991 (cladding + window) is cool green daylight; DSC03223 is unedited harsh midday; IMG_2819 (fog) is neutral grey. Side by side on one page they read as four different companies.

**The target grade — one recipe, applied to every keeper (Lightroom mobile free tier or Snapseed, ~90 seconds per frame):**

- White balance: warm the temperature until whites are just off-neutral — cream, not orange. If the wood looks like amber glass (current IMG_2403), pull back. The wood should look like wood at 4 pm, not like it's on fire.
- Highlights: −20 to −40. Nothing pure white except actual sky/steam.
- Shadows: lift +10 max. Keep shadows genuinely dark — the site ground is #0c0c0c and images must sink into it, not float on it.
- Saturation: −5 to −10 global. Then, if the tool allows, nudge greens toward −15 saturation — BC forest green at full chroma fights the warm-wood accent (visible in IMG_7991).
- Contrast: gentle S-curve or +10; matte finish (slightly raised black point) is on-brand, crushing blacks is not.
- Sharpening/clarity/dehaze: leave at zero. The grain overlay in CSS does the texture work.

**The one-line version:** *warm shadows, muted greens, no pure white, blacks that melt into the page.* This matches the brand doc's "warm-but-not-orange" rule and gives a new shoot a way to sit beside the best existing Alice Lake edits.

**Re-grade candidates:** IMG_2403 (pull the amber back) and DSC03223 (retire or replace — the light is unfixable; see §2).

### 1.4 Crops and art direction per breakpoint

Master files stay uncropped in Cloudinary; crops are transform-time (§3). Ratios by role:

| Role | Desktop ratio | Mobile ratio | Art direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | viewport (`100svh` cover) | viewport cover | `g_auto` is not enough for the hero — set a per-image gravity. Fog/landscape frames: `g_center`. Building-in-landscape: keep the building on a third; if the source has it centered, use `c_fill,g_auto:classic` and verify |
| Full-bleed plate | 21:9 (`ar_21:9`) | 4:5 (`ar_4:5`) | This is the real art-direction move: a plate is panoramic on desktop and *vertical* on mobile — not the same crop shrunk. Vertical crops re-center on the subject via `g_auto` |
| Pair (two-up) | 4:5 each | 4:5 stacked | Verticals always; pairs read as book spreads |
| Card / model grid | 3:2 (`ar_3:2`) | 3:2 | Matches existing `.card-image` 300px fixed height without letterboxing |
| Detail inset (text-adjacent) | 1:1 | 1:1 | Square detail crops beside prose — the El Croquis detail slot |
| Lightbox | native ratio, no crop | native | Full frame, `w_1600`+ |

### 1.5 Images per page and the sequencing rule

**The El Croquis rhythm, made a rule:** every page is a repeating unit of `E → words → D/A → words`, and **no two photographs may share a viewport with each other** (pairs count as one plate).

- **Budget:** Home 6–8 images; /saunas/ 1 establishing + 1 card image per model + 1 detail pair; /process/ exactly 5 (one P frame per step + one finished A frame); About 3–4; contact/booking 1. More than this and the plates stop being events.
- **Sequence:** open at E scale, end at D scale. A page that starts on the landscape and ends on grain says "we start with your site and end with the sanding." Never open on a detail; never end on a drone-scale wide.
- **One fog/landscape-only frame per page maximum** (an IMG_2819-class plate). It's the breath, not the meal.
- Full-bleed plates carry **no captions** (mood board rule: captions live in an index — see §6.3 plate numbering, which replaces captions with a quiet reference system).

## 4. The grid

### 4.1 Verdict on the current system

`.container` 1200 / `.wide-container` 1400 / 65ch prose / `body` 1920 is structurally sound and should **stay** — this is refinement, not rebuild. What's missing for the architecture-studio register is not different widths; it's (a) a *narrow* tier the mood board leans on constantly, (b) a defined relationship between full-bleed plates and the contained column, and (c) tokenized gutters (the nav's hardcoded `6rem` gutters are Jen's P1 #8).

### 4.2 The four-tier system (formalized)

```css
:root {
  --gutter: clamp(1.5rem, 5vw, 6rem);
  --measure-narrow: 34rem;   /* thesis/lede blocks — mood board --measure */
  --measure-prose: 65ch;     /* existing global p rule, unchanged */
  --width-content: 1200px;   /* .container, unchanged */
  --width-wide: 1400px;      /* .wide-container, unchanged */
  --width-frame: 1920px;     /* body frame, unchanged */
}
.container      { max-width: var(--width-content); padding-inline: var(--gutter); }
.wide-container { max-width: var(--width-wide);    padding-inline: var(--gutter); }
.narrow         { max-width: var(--measure-narrow); margin-inline: auto; }
nav .container  { padding-inline: var(--gutter); } /* replaces hardcoded 6rem, styles.css:260 */
```

The `.narrow` tier is the register change. Nakashima/Norm pages read as a thin ribbon of text between huge images; the mood board's every text section is `min(100% - gutter*2, 34rem)`. Thesis statements, ledes, and the process-page step copy go in `.narrow`; spec grids and model cards stay at 1200; galleries at 1400.

### 4.3 Columns and gutters

Inside `.container`: **12 columns, 24px gutters** as the mental model (CSS stays grid-template with semantic fractions; no col-* class system — this site doesn't need framework scaffolding). Standard splits: 12 (prose band), 6/6 (feature + text), 4/4/4 (cards), 8/4 (content + aside/plate-number margin — see §6.3). The existing `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(280px,1fr))` card grids already produce these; keep them.

**Mobile grid:** single column, `--gutter` resolving to 1.5rem, everything stacks in source order; pairs stack vertical (already in `.pair` auto-fit); the only two-up that survives below 768px is the spec table.

### 4.4 Full-bleed plates vs. the contained grid

Rule: **plates break the grid completely (100vw within the 1920 frame); text never does.** No half-bleed, no image-poking-out-of-column tricks except the one existing `.feature-image--overflow` move, which should be kept but capped: at 769–1024px reduce to `width: 130%; margin-left: -30%` (Jen's §6 finding). The alternation *contained text ribbon → full-bleed plate* IS the compression-and-release principle; anything in between muddies it.

Plate spacing: plates get `margin-block: 0` and sections around them keep the `clamp(6rem, 16vh, 12rem)` rhythm from the mood board — the plate touches the dark ground directly, no card, no border, no radius, no shadow.

## 6. Graphic elements — the non-photographic vocabulary

Everything here passes the restraint test by carrying information. Four elements, and a kill list.

### 6.1 Hairline rules — the primary graphic device

The mood board already runs on 1px rules (`--rule: #2a2724` — note: *warm* dark, not the site's cool `#2a2a2a`; adopt the warm value as `--color-border-subtle` replacement). Rules do structural work: separating spec rows, topping specimen blocks, framing the reference index. They are the web equivalent of drawing-sheet rulings in an El Croquis plan page. Usage: `border-top: 1px solid var(--rule)` on list rows and section indices; the 1px `gap` trick on `.pair`/`.swatches` grids (image pairs separated by a hairline of ground color reads as a book spread). **No decorative rules** — a rule with nothing on both sides of it is atmosphere; cut.

### 6.2 The eyebrow + index-mark system

The mood board's `.eyebrow` (0.68rem, 0.2em tracking, uppercase, `#c4a57b`) is the wayfinding element: one per section, always naming what the section *is* ("The scroll", "Photography"). Extend it with **section indices** on the process page: `01 — Consultation`, `02 — Design & Planning`, etc., the numeral set in Cormorant at display size against `--ink-faint`, content in the 8/4 grid split beside it. Numbers-as-structure is the architectural convention (sheet numbers, detail callouts) and it does real work on a page whose whole job is "here is the sequence."

### 6.3 Plate numbering

The mood board rule says plates get no captions. The information doesn't vanish — it moves to a quiet reference: each full-bleed plate carries a small margin mark, `Plate 04 — S6, Brackendale, 2025`, set in the utility style (0.66rem, tracked, `--ink-faint`), bottom-left under the plate, inside the container margin. This is the El Croquis credits block miniaturized. It replaces captions with provenance — *which build, where, when* — which is evidence, not description. One line, ~40 characters max, sourced from real build data only (never invented; where the build/date is unknown, the plate runs unmarked).

## 7. Anti-convergence check

Where this design risks looking like every other dark-mode premium site: dark ground + serif display + fade-up-on-scroll + full-bleed photography *is* the 2026 template. Squarespace ships a theme within shouting distance of it. The register alone is not the identity. What makes it unmistakably SSC:

1. **The uncaptioned plate + provenance mark system (§6.3).** No template does captionless plates with El Croquis-style provenance lines. It requires having real builds to cite — which is exactly why it can't be faked by a competitor with stock photography.
2. **Process photography with Lee's actual hands (§1, P-series).** Storm and Voyageur structurally cannot shoot P4 (dry-fitting a joint) because they don't build that way. The P-series is the moat; everything else is furniture.
3. **The warm neutral ramp** (`#9a9590` secondary, `#2a2724` rules). Template dark modes are cool-grey. A page where even the borders are warm reads subliminally different — it's the 40/60 duality executed at the token level.
4. **The badge in the footer only.** Every template puts the logo top-left at 40px. A site that identifies itself by wordmark and *signs itself* at the end with a seal behaves like a monograph, not a website.
5. **The 21:9 → 4:5 art-directed crop flip (§1.4).** Responsive images that *re-compose* rather than shrink are still rare; mobile users get vertical plates composed for their frame, which is why Lee liked the mood board's mobile feel.
6. **Fog as punctuation (§1.5).** The one-landscape-per-page ration turns IMG_2819-class frames into a recurring rest beat — a pacing signature, not a hero cliché.
7. **What stays deleted:** no scroll-lock, no parallax, no infinite zoom, no glowing CTAs (Lee's explicit line), no icon rows, no gradient meshes. Restraint at this register is itself distinguishing, because the competitors decorate.

Residual risk, named honestly: Cormorant Garamond on dark is having a moment across premium wellness brands. The type alone will not differentiate — the photography system and provenance vocabulary have to. That's Beatrice's lane to push further; flagged for her.
